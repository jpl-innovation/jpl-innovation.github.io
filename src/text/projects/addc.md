## Where it started

ADDC (Kiến Trúc An Dương) designs, builds and fits out homes, offices and showrooms in Ho Chi Minh City. Its website was a ready-made template: one long home page with a building-site photo slider, the same blocks every builder's site has, and nothing that showed how ADDC works or what a project costs.

![The old addc.vn home page: a building-site photo with the company name in capitals](/work/addc/before-desktop.webp)

## Three services, three pages

The new site splits ADDC's work the way clients think about it: architecture (Kiến trúc), construction (Thi công) and interiors (Nội thất), each on its own page with its process, its prices and its own 3D. A home page tells the story once; a projects page and an about page hold the record.

- **Architecture**: the house as a line drawing, a clay model or in real materials, cut as a plan or a section, with the facade's colour chosen live.
- **Construction**: seven stages of a townhouse going up, driven by the scroll, from piling to handover, with the temporary works shown or hidden.
- **Interiors**: a room the visitor furnishes: three spaces, five floors, five wall colours, wood, fabric and light.

![The construction page: the townhouse at stage five, scaffolding and safety netting on its face](/work/addc/build.webp)

## Built to feel finished

- A loader that tells the company's story in four photos (drawing, site, finished room, groundbreaking) while the page really loads, and opens into the first picture.
- Every picture can be opened large, growing out of its own spot.
- Titles rise into place, photos wait in a dark frame instead of a pale card, and the 3D is prepared before it is seen, so scrolling stays smooth.
- 31 3D models of the materials and furniture ADDC works with: concrete, steel, brick, stone, timber, tiles, a townhouse in seven stages and the rooms inside it.

![The interiors page: a furnished living room in 3D, with the visitor's choices of floor, walls and fabric beside it](/work/addc/room.webp)

## How it was made

The content came from ADDC itself: 161 photos from its own sites and projects, its licence and equipment, its price tables and its site diary. Every page was checked at desktop and phone widths, every section scrolled through, and the frame rate measured on a 240 Hz screen before anything went live.
